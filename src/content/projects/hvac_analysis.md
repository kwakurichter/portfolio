## Overview

This project analyzed alternative heating options for Richters Herbs, a greenhouse operation in southern Ontario. The existing system used 14 propane-fired unit heaters, and rising propane prices and carbon costs created a need to evaluate lower-cost heating alternatives.

## Site Survey

The project began with a full survey of the greenhouse facilities. I documented dimensions, materials, door types, equipment locations, heater nameplates, and the different temperature zones across the site.

The facility included:

- A propagation zone requiring a 60 deg F minimum ambient temperature.
- A large greenhouse zone requiring a 50 deg F minimum ambient temperature.
- A boiler room and support spaces heated by adjacent structures.

## Modeling

After the survey, I created a 2D AutoCAD floorplan and a 1:1 Fusion 360 model of the greenhouse structures. These models were used to estimate surface areas, volumes, material boundaries, and heating loads.

Heating loads were calculated at exterior temperatures of 15 deg F, 23 deg F, and 32 deg F using ASHRAE-based load calculation methods.

## Findings

The existing propane system cost approximately $110,909 per year, or about $924 per day over a four-month heating season.

The proposed system used 24 Lennox ELP/ELS120 heat pumps:

- 20 units for the remaining greenhouse zone.
- 2 units for Greenhouse #1.
- 2 units for Greenhouse #3.

The heat pump system was estimated to consume about 440,000 kWh annually. Including backup propane operation for very cold days, the proposed annual operating cost was estimated at $64,920.

## Result

The analysis estimated annual operating savings of about $45,989 compared with the legacy propane system. Assuming a conservative installed cost of $240,000, the projected break-even period was about 5.5 years.
