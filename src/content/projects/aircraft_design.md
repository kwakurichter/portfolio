## Overview

This University of Waterloo aircraft design project developed a conceptual aircraft configuration from initial requirements through sizing, layout, aerodynamic analysis, and performance checks.

## Scope

The report covered:

- Initial design requirements and mission profile assumptions.
- Weight, wing loading, and thrust-to-weight estimates.
- Fuselage layout and internal subsystem packaging.
- Powerplant selection.
- Wing, flap, and airfoil decisions.
- Center of gravity and longitudinal stability.
- Tail sizing and landing gear configuration.
- Performance analysis and scale model considerations.

## Design Work

The design process moved from high-level requirements into configuration tradeoffs and iterative calculations. Major aerodynamic decisions included the use of a NACA 65(2)-415 airfoil reference, low-wing configuration, high-lift devices, and a conventional tail arrangement.

## Outcome

The project strengthened my ability to connect conceptual requirements to engineering estimates, physical layout, and performance consequences across a full aircraft-level system.
