## Overview

The Gripper Robot was a third-year mechanical design project at the University of Waterloo. The goal was to design, build, test, and verify a pick-and-place manipulator capable of moving objects between specified pickup and drop-off areas.

## Requirements

The manipulator had to operate from a fixed base, move objects without direct user contact, and avoid mobile or launching designs. Our team also focused on maximizing strength-to-weight ratio and placement accuracy while minimizing cost.

## Design

The final design used a rotating base, a horizontal boom, a vertical lift, and a mechanical claw. The claw used a power-screw actuation concept because early prototypes showed high grip strength, simple construction, and easy integration with the boom.

The vertical lift was redesigned from a cylindrical rod and slider to a linear rail to reduce binding under load.

## Manufacturing

The rotating base used machined aluminum for strength and weight. Structural elements used laser-cut acrylic sheets, and complex or non-structural components were 3D printed to reduce cost and manufacturing time.

The final material cost was $265.57.

## Results

The finished robot lifted objects more than 2 inches, achieved a strength-to-weight ratio of 2.44:1, and repeatedly placed objects in the target drop-off area with more than 98% precision.

Out of 23 competing teams, the project won first place for design.
