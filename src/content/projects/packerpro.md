## Overview

PackerPro was my final-year mechanical engineering design project at the University of Waterloo. The project was sponsored by Richters Herbs, and the goal was to automate a manual seed-packing process used across more than 1000 seed varieties.

The machine accepts bulk seeds and empty packets, portions the seeds, fills each packet, seals it, and ejects the finished packet into a collection area.

## Problem

Richters Herbs relied on manual labour for a production process that had become difficult to scale. Operators had to retrieve a seed variety, measure seeds by weight or volume, fill plastic packets, and seal batches with a heat press.

The design requirements included:

- Repeatable seed measuring within 10 wt. percent of target.
- Packet filling and heat sealing.
- Finished packet collection.
- Minimal human interaction during production.
- A user-friendly operating process.
- Cleanout between seed varieties.

## Design

The final concept used a rotary table with four packet clamps moving around a stationary plate. Four major operations were mounted 90 degrees apart so the system could process packets in sequence:

- Packet injection.
- Seed portioning.
- Heat sealing.
- Packet ejection.

The full machine also included a pneumatic circuit, a 12 V electrical system, stepper motor drivers, MOSFET boards, a servo-controlled seed valve, and an Arduino microcontroller.

## Manufacturing

The project combined machined aluminum, waterjet-cut structural components, 3D-printed parts, pneumatic actuators, electrical boards, fasteners, and off-the-shelf controls hardware. My individual contributions included stakeholder relations, mechanical design and manufacturing, and electrical system leadership.

The final material cost was $1358.19.

## Results

PackerPro produced packets at a rate of 15 packets per minute. Verification testing showed a standard deviation of 0.963 in seed portioning tests, and the final system was able to produce roughly 150 to 200 packets in a run with a simple loading and start process.

The project won the Best Prototype Award out of 42 competing capstone teams and was nominated for Best Overall.
